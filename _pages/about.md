Hello! I am a Ph.D. candidate in Political Science at Stanford University, specializing in comparative politics and quantitative methods. My research examines democratic backsliding, political elites, and political accountability, with a regional focus on Middle East. My dissertation studies how democratic backsliding transforms political competition from within, examining how political leaders reshape legislatures, elite behavior, and state institutions as democratic constraints erode. More broadly, I study how institutions and social conditions shape who enters politics, how elites reproduce political power, and how citizens evaluate government performance. My work draws on large-scale administrative and text data, and quasi-experimental research designs to study these questions across national and local politics.


I am a Ric Weiland Graduate Fellow at Stanford University. I am also a junior fellow of the Association for Analytic Learning about Islam and Muslim Societies (AALIMS). My research is supported by the King Center on Global Development, Freeman Spogli Institute for International Studies, Abbasi Program in Islamic Studies, and Markaz Resource Center. At Stanford, I co-organize Comparative Politics Graduate Students Workshop in Political Science Department, and serve as a Political Science graduate Student Association (PSGA) co-president, and Abbasi Program Graduate Student Network Coordinator.

Before beginning my graduate studies at Stanford, I earned my B.A. and M.A. in Political Science from Bogazici University in Istanbul.
